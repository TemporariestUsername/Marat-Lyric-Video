Born again nightly in the printed word,
every issue a torch, every column heard,
I name the names that the salon protects —
hoarders! deserters! royalists! suspects!
They sent their dogs, so I went below,
wrote by sewer-light in filth and shadow,
came up burning with a skinful of sores —
and the fever I carry is about to be YOURS!

I am the anger — the JUST anger — of the people,
that's why they listen... that's why they BELIEVE...

September at the gates, and the prisons packed,
the enemy WITHIN — I let the righteous act!
I lit the lamp, the pikes did the math,
three days of red arithmetic — an Abbaye bath.
My columns take the pulse — read the fever's signs!
I'm a DOCTOR, I was healing 'fore I wrote a line!
A surgeon knows what the merciful forgot:
you bleed the body NOW — or you watch it ROT!

France, five or six hundred heads cut off
would have assured your repose... your freedom... your happiness.
But a false humanity held back your arms —
and for that mercy,
it will cost the lives of MILLIONS of your brothers!
I WEEP FOR YOU! DON'T YOU SEE THAT I WEEP?!

Citizen Marat. You stand before the Revolutionary Tribunal.
The charge: incitement to murder and pillage. How do you answer?

I answer: I AM THE EYES OF THE REVOLUTION —
you don't gouge them out for what they SEE!

A fire's in my flesh where the sewers kissed,
vinegar rag on my skull, acid words on my lips as I persist —
soaking in the only mercy I allow myself,
still writing, still naming, still the people's health.

Who's that? A girl from Caen with a list to share?
Ah, traitors. Please... sit right there.
Give me those names — it's the truth I seek —
Yes... they'll all be guillotined by the end of the week.

Friend of the People, why the hush?
Oh so pale, though your bath does blush.
You thought your paper would save the nation's life.
I saved a hundred thousand at a one-man price.
One last operation, Doctor... feel the sting.
Although, I'll admit you were right about one thing...

You're going to need a tub
for all that blood.
